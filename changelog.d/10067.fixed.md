PEC shape gradients (`Box`, `Sphere`, `Cylinder`, `PolySlab`, `TriangleMesh` with `PECMedium`) now use an exterior stress "collar" around the structure instead of sampling the fields on the PEC surface: the adjoint gradient magnitudes match finite differences to a few percent on spheres, boxes, and polyslabs where the surface-sampled gradients were previously off by factors of 2-15. PEC structures in a 2D simulation use the same collar, one layer of cells in the simulation plane. A wall next to PEC that tilts out of a 2D simulation's plane (an angled `PolySlab` sidewall with the slab axis normal to the plane) takes the force on its in-plane normal, like a dielectric's 2D boundary integral, so its vertex gradient no longer depends on the tilt. A `PolySlab` traced on both its `vertices` and its `sidewall_angle` now takes the angle gradient from the vertices' sidewall samples instead of sampling the same walls twice, which split a PEC collar's forces between the two sets and made both gradients too small; an angle traced alone reads the same sidewall samples, so in a 2D simulation containing the slab axis its gradient no longer depends on whether the vertices are traced as well, and is no longer taken from wall strips that sample the slab off the simulation plane. The collar stops at absorbing and periodic domain boundaries, so a structure running into the PML or across a periodic boundary keeps it; sources and objective monitors next to the surface, and in a simulation with symmetry their mirror images, are cut out of the collar instead of disabling it, with a warning giving the share of the surface whose gradient loses accuracy (a monitor plane crossing a structure costs little, one close above its surface much more). A PEC or PMC domain boundary next to the surface is treated like neighboring metal, so a structure close to one (a line above a PEC-boundary ground plane) keeps its collar too. A dielectric structure that meets a PEC, including a dielectric sheet whose edge meets a PEC sheet, now takes its gradient at the shared surface from the same collar, so a shared dielectric/PEC boundary gives the same gradient whichever structure it is traced on. Zero-thickness PEC sheets (`PEC2D`) are now sampled at the local grid step like bulk PEC; a `PolySlab` sheet's edges were sampled a tenth of a wavelength apart, which halved its shape gradients. A hole cut out of a PEC sheet with a `ClipOperation` difference now has a shape gradient; its edges were treated as off the metal surface and got none. The radius gradient of a `Sphere` or `Cylinder` hole cut out of a PEC with a `ClipOperation` difference accounts for the collar lying inside the curved surface, where the stretch of a growing radius shrinks with depth instead of growing. Geometry gradients of a non-penetrable `LossyMetalMedium` under the default surface impedance subpixel method now use the same collar as PEC; they previously went through the dielectric formula with the metal's bulk permittivity, which the solver never resolves, and could be off by orders of magnitude and in sign. Under the `Staircasing` or `VolumetricAveraging` lossy-metal methods the solver treats the metal as a regular conductor, so it keeps the dielectric formula. Metal on a substrate or between unequal dielectric layers keeps the in-plane force at its edges: the collar dropped every stress jump between cells of different permittivity, which put the width and length gradients of strips and patches on a substrate an order of magnitude too small or flipped their sign (an RF patch antenna was 74° from finite differences, now about 2°); it now drops only the normal part of the jump where the interface is flat. On an anisotropic grid the collar is sized by the cells across the surface instead of the widest cell along it, so it stays a few fine cells deep. Before any simulation runs, collection also warns when metal sits within a few cells in front of a traced PEC surface (a narrow gap, an inside corner, a nearby structure or domain wall) or when the surface curves within the collar's depth (a radius of curvature under about five grid cells), with the share of the surface affected, since the gradient loses accuracy there. The collar now forms the stress of anisotropic media, including a uniform anisotropic `CustomMedium`, with their full permittivity tensor and the unsymmetrized electric stress, which is divergence-free in any reciprocal medium; it previously used the mean of the diagonal, which put individual gradient components of a PEC plate in an anisotropic layer off by up to 3x. Collection also warns when the collar holds media its stress does not model: a spatially varying medium, a non-reciprocal (time-modulated or magneto-optic) medium, or a non-PEC 2D sheet, including one lying on a grid plane. Collection raises an `AdjointError` instead of running out of memory when the grid box a collar is staged from would need more than `config.adjoint.memory_allotment_fraction` of the available memory (a PEC structure spanning hundreds of cells per side).
